How far is care for rural Sindh?

Road travel time from 5,159 villages to the nearest health facility, and a measurement of what open geospatial data leaves out. Built on open data alone, the estimate doubles travel times and counts ten times as many people as underserved.

Scope and method

Reachable is not the same as available

The index estimates physical reachability. It says nothing about whether the facility is staffed, stocked or open when needed.

Answered

1. Reachable

Can a person get there?

Village-to-facility travel time over the OpenStreetMap road network, with speeds set for rural road classes.

Partly

2. Physically present

Does the building exist?

Satellite check of a stratified sample: 42 of 50 geocodes had a plausible facility structure within 500 m (84%, 95% CI 71.5–91.7).

Not answered

3. Operational

Is it staffed, stocked, open?

Needs ground data. Imagery can confirm a roof, not a doctor.

How the numbers were made

Travel time from every village was computed with one multi-source Dijkstra traversal seeded from all facility nodes, over per-district OSM drive networks with a 15 km halo so edge villages can reach facilities across district lines. An independent routing check on a long rural journey agreed within 3%.

PPHI Sindh publishes its facility lists as PDFs with names but no coordinates. These were parsed and geocoded by matching each facility's settlement name against gazetteers, constrained to its own district and verified against district polygons.

  • Facilities sit at settlement centroids, typically 0–2 km from the building.
  • Scenario B is still an undercount, worst in Tando Allahyar, Tando Muhammad Khan and Umerkot.
  • Speeds are judgement, not survey. Both scenarios use the same speeds, so the comparison holds.

Paper and citation

Read and cite the paper

Published in the International Journal of Advanced Research, Volume 3, Issue 5, September 2026.

Open geospatial data is used routinely to study access to care in low- and middle-income countries, but its completeness for rural health facilities had not been checked against an authoritative register.

This study does that for Sindh and finds that open data omits most rural public primary care. Analyses built on it risk mistaking misreported populations for underserved ones.
